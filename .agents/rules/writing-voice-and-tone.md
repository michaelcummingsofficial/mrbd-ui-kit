All user-facing copy (marketing pages, emails, in-app strings, metadata, CTAs) follows one voice: calm, confident, plain, and short. Think of how Apple writes a product page: one idea per sentence, concrete words, and the confidence to stop. State the benefit and let it land. When writing or editing any copy, apply these rules.

### Short sentences, one idea each

Write the way Apple does. A sentence carries one idea and ends. Three short sentences beat one long one with clauses. Hero sub text is two or three sentences and under about 30 words. Section intros are one or two sentences.

Before: "A powerful, flexible platform that lets teams of any size plan, track, and deliver their work across every project, with reporting built in and no setup required."

After: "Plan the work. Track it. Ship it. Reporting is built in, and there is nothing to set up."

### Concrete verbs and nouns

Say what the person does, not what the product is. "Send the file" beats "file sharing functionality." "Book a room" beats "reservation capabilities." Lists of three verbs read well; lists of five features do not.

### One emphasis, if any

A paragraph may carry one phrase in ink for emphasis, the way Apple bolds a single claim inside gray body copy. Never two. Never a whole sentence in bold.

### No jargon above the fold

Industry and technical terms are fine on detail pages and in the docs. They do not belong in a hero, a section title, or a card heading. Say what the term means for the reader instead: "your data never leaves your account" rather than "end-to-end encrypted at rest."

### No em dashes

Never use `—` (em dash) in copy. It reads as machine-written. Replace it with the punctuation a person would actually use:

- Period when the two halves are full thoughts
- Comma when one half depends on the other
- Colon when the second half explains or lists

This applies to copy only. Standalone `—` as a "no value" marker in data tables is fine, and code comments are out of scope.

### Confidence, not contempt

State a benefit calmly. Never oversell with absolutes or drama. Never name or mock the enemy. No stacked adjectives: one is plenty, none is often better.

### Calm CTAs

Short, lowercase-feeling verbs. Two or three words where possible ("Get started", "See pricing", "Try it free"). No shouting, no exclamation points.

### Clean sentences

Write parallel, complete sentences. Don't stack fragments and dashes for drama. Read it aloud; if you run out of breath, split it.
